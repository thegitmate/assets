Executive summary

DIAG Aviation Technik is a Part 145 MRO formed in 2025 by merging two maintenance operations, now working across roughly fifty light and business aircraft types from several manufacturers. Across our call you pointed to several places where time and information slip away, so this proposal sets out the one we recommend, plus three other things you raised that we can help with.

Everything here is independent. We recommend looking at the maintenance knowledge assistant first, because it targets the pain you could measure most clearly and shows a return fastest. But the other three each stand on their own: take any one, in any combination, with or without the assistant. There is no package to buy into, and no reason not to start small.

What we heard

Four themes came out of the call, each mapping to something we can build.

Finding information across manuals and parts catalogues. Technicians look up procedures and parts across manuals that differ by airframe, engine and propeller. You estimated one to two hours per person per day across five to ten technicians. This is the most measurable pain, and what we recommend addressing first.

Finalizing work reports. You called this your single biggest time item: writing styles vary across an international team, entries that sometimes lack detail, and a manual pass to check that parts are booked and wording is appropriate before release.

Reactivating dormant customers. Your new CEO wants to review the last one to two years of projects to flag recurring work, for example prompting an owner when an annual is due.

Recording offline meetings. You raised the wish to record mostly offline meetings and generate structured notes so actions are not forgotten weeks later.

What we recommend: a Maintenance Knowledge Assistant

Our recommended starting point

A maintenance assistant built on your own documentation. It lets a technician find the right part and the applicable procedure in seconds: they ask in plain language across manuals that differ by airframe, engine and propeller, and get the answer with the exact source document and page to verify. The result is what we call an augmented technician, the same expert judgement with the right page open in seconds. This is the system we demonstrated live on the call.

  • Find the right reference in seconds. A technician asks in plain language and gets the part or procedure with its source document and page, verifying on the spot instead of searching across manuals. Faster lookups mean fewer interruptions and more tasks completed in a day.
  • Answers your team can trust. Every answer points to its source, and when the documentation does not contain the answer the assistant says so rather than inventing one. Your team stays accountable and audit ready, which in maintenance matters more than anything else.
  • Your technicians stay in control. The tool speeds the search; the technician reads the source and decides. Especially valuable as you bring two former teams onto one shared way of working across many aircraft types.

On document ingestion, we load the manuals you already use and, where helpful, assist with semi automated collection from provider sources, with a person confirming what gets added. Where your manuals sit in a provider system such as Veryon, how we access and export them is a key dependency, which we work through as part of the setup, since licensing and export options vary by provider, and we design the ingestion around what is technically and contractually possible.

Indicative investmentsetup 2,500 €, monthly subscription 350 to 450 €. On these assumptions, to confirm together: up to 10 technicians, and an initial library of roughly 150 to 200 manual sets (airframe, engine, propeller) expanded as your coverage grows. "We host" means the assistant runs as a secure online service, with nothing to install or maintain on your side; the setup fee covers the one time work of ingesting your documents and configuring the system. Other ways we can help

Each of the following stands entirely on its own. You can take any one, with or without the knowledge assistant, in any combination. We have ordered them by the value we think they create for you, starting with the work report assistant.

Work Report Assistant

You raised report finalisation as one of your biggest time items. Rather than write reports for your mechanics, the assistant reviews the report a mechanic has already drafted: it checks the work described against the relevant manuals, points out what is missing or inconsistent, and suggests specific edits. Where useful it can produce a clean, consistent rewrite in DIAG's own style, learned from a sample of your best reports, for the mechanic to check and finalise. The mechanic stays the author and signs off, so the human judgement you value is kept while the review time and the slips are not. We will shape the exact checks around how your reports are written today and what tends to go wrong, which we will work through with you.

Indicative investmentfrom 2,000 € setup, monthly 350 to 450 €. On these assumptions, to confirm together: a sample of past reports to learn your style, access to the manuals the assistant should check against, and the report format you use today; optionally a link to your parts or booking system if you want that checked too. Covering your current technician team.

Dormant Customer Reactivation

This supports the review your new CEO already wants to run. The system reads your historical projects, surfaces recurring and due work, and prompts a specific, timely follow up: flagging an owner whose annual is approaching, or a recurring snag worth a proactive call before it becomes an AOG. It turns project history you already hold into booked maintenance, without anyone combing through old files by hand.

Indicative investmentfrom 1,500 € setup, monthly 150 to 400 €. On these assumptions, to confirm together: access to the last one to two years of project history in an exportable form, and agreement on which triggers (annuals, recurring tasks) you want surfaced.

Meeting Capture and Notes

The smallest, lowest risk way to start working together. It records your mostly offline meetings and produces structured notes tailored to the meeting type, so decisions and actions are not lost weeks later. We are upfront that capable note taking is widely available off the shelf; what we add is the fit to how you actually run meetings, and a low commitment way to see how we operate before any larger build.
